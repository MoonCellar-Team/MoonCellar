Left column of the overview row · 760px · The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt

MoonCellar note:

Widely treated as the high-water mark of the open-world role-playing game, and the argument is mostly about writing: side quests here have consequences that outlast their own cutscenes. You do not need the first two games to follow it, though importing a save changes a few early scenes. The expansions are stronger than the base campaign's middle act — Hearts of Stone is the tighter story, Blood and Wine the better-looking world — and playing them after the ending, rather than during, keeps the pacing intact. Fifty hours for the main story is a conservative figure; most people need more.

Summary:

You are Geralt of Rivia, a mercenary monster slayer hired to find the Child of Prophecy — a girl who may hold the key to saving the world. As war rages across the Northern Kingdoms, every contract, every conversation and every side quest pulls the same thread: what will you do for someone you have sworn to protect?

C · The lists panel

One row, eight columns at the page's full width. Eight is what the endpoint returns, and the column count follows it down the breakpoints — four from 1250px, two below 768px — so the row is never ragged and never wider than the cards need. Every card is the shared ListCard tile: a 2×2 mosaic of the first covers, the game count in the corner, the name clamped to two lines, then the author and the likes.
