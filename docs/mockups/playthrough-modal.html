Playthrough modal

Three ways to lay out the modal where a player records a playthrough: its category, platform, finish date, hours, the Mastered flag (shown only for Completed, always next to the category), a comment and who can see it. All three keep a visible label on every field and share the new date field, which shows a DD.MM.YYYY mask and accepts typed input as well as the calendar.

A · Labelled form

The current form, tidied

Same order and controls as today, so it is the smallest change to build.

  • Category and Platform share one row; Date and Game time share the next.
  • Hours carry a unit suffix instead of putting it in the label.
  • Mastered is a labelled switch between Category and Platform, in the same row, and the slot closes when the category is not Completed.
  • Show on the game page and Contains spoilers sit under a "Visibility" heading.
  • Delete moves to the right edge, away from Save, as a text button.

B · Status chips

Category as chips

The six categories are always visible as chips in the site's status colours, so switching is one tap instead of opening a list.

  • The chip colours match the status badges on game cards.
  • Mastered is a half-chip welded to the selected Completed chip, in the mastered yellow; it only grows out of Completed and disappears with any other choice.
  • Off, the half-chip turns grey and keeps the star, so it still reads as something to turn on.
  • Visibility toggles and the buttons live in a footer that stays pinned while the comment scrolls.
  • Fits a phone: the chips wrap and the date row folds to two columns.

C · Details and notes

Two columns on desktop

The resizable desktop modal is wide enough for a details column beside the comment, so the editor gets the full height instead of sitting under the fields. On a phone the column stacks above the editor and the layout matches A.

  • A one-line summary under the tabs reads back what is saved: status, platform, date, hours.
  • Mastered is a strip attached under the Category field, a star button that fills when on; the strip is part of the field and folds away for any category but Completed.
  • The details column holds every short field and the visibility switches.
  • The comment area grows with the modal when it is resized.

B on a phone

Category on a phone

At 360px the seven pills wrap into three ragged rows: the Completed and Mastered pair is wider than its neighbours, so each row ends at a different place and the right side of the panel stays empty. Four ways to keep the category and Mastered together without the ragged edge.

Category

1 · Even grid

Three equal columns, two rows, so every row ends at the same edge. Completed sits at the end of the first row and Mastered hangs under it as a tab, between the rows.

Fits: all six visible, no empty space. Cost: the grid gets a third row only while Completed is picked.

Category

2 · Two columns, star inside

Two equal columns, three rows. Mastered shrinks to a star at the right end of the Completed cell, so the cell keeps its size whether the star is there or not.

Fits: nothing moves when Completed is picked. Cost: the star has no text; it needs a tooltip and an aria-label.

Category

3 · One scrolling line

The desktop row, kept on one line and scrolled sideways, with the right edge fading out. The picked category scrolls into view when the modal opens.

Fits: one line of height, same look as desktop. Cost: Played, Backlog and Dropped are off screen until the row is swiped.

Category
Completed

4 · Dropdown below 520px

Under the phone breakpoint the pills become a dropdown with the status dot, and Mastered becomes a button beside it in the same row. Desktop keeps the pills.

Fits: the smallest block, matches Platform below it. Cost: two renders of one field, and changing the category takes two taps.
