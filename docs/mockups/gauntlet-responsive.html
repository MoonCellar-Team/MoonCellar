Gauntlet Responsive Layouts

The wheel is the point of the page, yet at 1280×800 the mode panel takes half the height and the wheel shrinks to about 400px, while the winner block is short enough that its details need scrolling. Both directions below fold the mode panel into a single toolbar row and move the "how it works" copy into the empty winner state, so the wheel gets the full height that is left. Each is drawn at its real size and scaled to fit: 1920×1080, 1280×800, a 768×1024 tablet and a 390×844 phone, all after a spin with the winner shown.

A · Split stage B · Wheel first, winner drawer
Option A

Split stage

The wheel and the winner share the screen at full height, side by side on desktop and stacked on tablets and phones.

  • The toolbar holds the title, the mode switch with match counts, the applied filters and the Filters and Lists buttons; the old panel's lede and steps live in the empty winner block.
  • The wheel's size is the height left under the toolbar: about 900px at 1080p and 640px at 1280×800, up from 400px.
  • The winner block always reaches the bottom of the screen, so cover, name, actions, summary and facts fit without scrolling at 1280×800; anything longer scrolls inside the block.
  • On a tablet the wheel spans the width and the winner follows below it; on a phone a sticky Spin bar keeps the action in reach.
Option B

Wheel first, winner drawer

The wheel owns the page; the winner arrives in a full-height drawer from the right, or a bottom sheet on phones, after the spin.

  • Before a spin nothing competes with the wheel: it is centred and as tall as the space under the toolbar.
  • The drawer is the full window height, so the winner's details never need scrolling on desktop; Spin again and Open game sit in it.
  • On a phone the winner is a bottom sheet that can be dragged up for the details, the same pattern as the site's popovers.
  • The trade-off: the wheel is partly covered while a winner is shown, and History is the only place to see the previous result.

The wheel, cover and background are drawn placeholders. Colours, spacing and radii are the project's tokens, generated into the :root block by bun --filter web sync:tokens. Hanken Grotesk stands in for ApercuPro, whose licensed file cannot be served from a third-party host.
