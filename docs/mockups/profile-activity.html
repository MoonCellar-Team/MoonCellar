Profile activity

The Activity section at the bottom of a profile. Several layouts that cut the empty space of the current timeline and put the delete action next to the entry it removes. Every frame can be switched to phone width and to a visitor's view, where delete is not rendered.

A tall row for one line of textThe 80 px cover is 107 px high, while most entries are a single 20 px sentence — four fifths of each row is empty.

Delete lives at the far edgeTime and × sit in their own column at the right of a wide panel, detached from the sentence, and × is invisible until hover.

Round 2 · full-size covers

Keep the 80 px cover, fill the row

The covers stay the size they are today. Instead of shrinking the row, these options use the whole width of the panel: the entries flow into a grid or columns, and the text is structured to fill the cover's height.

D

Cover grid

The cover is the entry, and the grid runs continuously across days. A day is a cell of its own, the size of a cover, so a new day never starts a half-empty row. Delete is a round × on the cover's corner.

Activity

Gains

  • Every row is full: day cells and tiles flow together.
  • Six to seven entries per row on desktop, three on a phone.
  • × sits on the thing it deletes.

Costs

  • Platform and hours do not fit a tile and are left out.
  • Chronology reads left to right, like a gallery.
E

Structured cards

Cards in a continuous grid of two or three columns, without day breaks: the day moves into the time label (Yesterday, 22:14). The text is split into a status chip, the game name as a title, and platform, hours and date as chips, so it is about as tall as the cover. Delete is a × in the card's top right corner.

Activity

Gains

  • No row is left half-empty, because days no longer break the grid.
  • The cover height is filled with content.
  • × is at a fixed spot on every card and always visible.

Costs

  • Days are harder to scan without headers.
  • Entries with a single fact (Wishlist) still leave some room inside the card.
F

Day rail with cards

Days in a left rail, the entries of each day as cards in two columns to the right of it. An odd last card spans both columns, so each day block closes flush with the right edge.

Activity

Gains

  • Day grouping stays, and each day block fills the width.
  • The rail labels the day once, whatever the number of entries.

Costs

  • A day with one entry becomes a single wide card with room inside it.
  • Below about 760 px the cards fall back to one column.
G

Headline entries

Entries laid out like small articles in a continuous grid of two or three columns, without card backgrounds. The game name is a headline with the status chip, details are chips, and a footer with the day, time and a Delete text link lines up with the cover's bottom edge.

Activity

Gains

  • The grid runs across days, so every row is full.
  • Lighter than E: hairlines instead of filled cards.
  • Delete is a labelled action next to the time, clearly tied to its entry.

Costs

  • The tallest entries of this round, about 120 px each.
  • A labelled Delete on every entry adds visual noise for the owner.

Round 1 · compact

Earlier options

The first set shrinks the covers to save height.

A

Compact rows

The smallest step from today. A 40 px cover carries the status as a coloured edge, the row is one hover target, and time and delete sit together at its end. Delete asks for confirmation inside the row.

Activity

Gains

  • Row height drops from ~107 px to ~56 px; a page of 20 fits in about one screen.
  • Hover lights the whole row, so × clearly belongs to it.
  • No modal: Delete / Cancel replace the time in place.

Costs

  • The vertical timeline line and dots go away.
  • On a wide panel the row is still long; time and × stay at the right edge, only closer through the hover band.
B

Two-column cards

Each entry is a small card, two or three per row depending on the panel width, which uses the width instead of stretching one line across it. Delete moves into a ⋯ menu in the card's corner, next to room for later actions.

Activity

Gains

  • Half the height of A on desktop.
  • The menu is always visible, so delete is discoverable on touch and with a mouse alike.
  • The menu can take "Open game" or "Copy link" later without new layout.

Costs

  • Two columns read left-to-right, then down — chronology is less obvious than in a single list.
  • A menu is one extra click before Delete.
C

Day rail

The densest option. Days move into a left rail, time becomes a fixed column, covers shrink to 32 px. Delete has a reserved slot at the row end and removes immediately with an Undo in place of the row.

Activity

Gains

  • Rows of ~44 px, and day headers stop costing a line of their own.
  • Fixed time and delete columns line up down the whole page — nothing floats at a random distance.
  • Undo instead of confirm: one click to delete, one to take it back.

Costs

  • Undo needs the delete to be deferred until the row is dismissed or the page changes.
  • 32 px covers are hard to recognise; the game name carries the row.
